`lh` now asks once, after a command's answer, whether to share anonymous usage data with your Lighthouse; nothing is sent until you say yes. Only `y` or `yes`, in any case, counts as a yes; Enter or anything else is a No that is kept for that Lighthouse and never sent. The question appears only when stdin, stdout and stderr are all a terminal (never in scripts, pipes or CI, and never under `DO_NOT_TRACK` set to anything but `0` or `false`), on stderr, so the command's output is unchanged. After a yes, `lh forecast manual` is counted as a manual forecast run from the command line. `lh` asks only after a command that reached Lighthouse and succeeded (never after help, `lh config`, `lh connection` or a refused command), only a Lighthouse that may be asked and labels the command line, and each Lighthouse on its own; a Lighthouse that does not answer about usage data costs at most a second, and the question waits for the next run. If your Lighthouse cannot record a yes, `lh` says so on stderr, keeps nothing and sends nothing, and the command's exit code is unchanged. Answers are kept in `usage-data.json` beside the command line's config, readable by you only and separate from the voter keys; when two terminals answer at once, the first answer given is kept, and an answers file `lh` cannot read is never written over: nothing is asked or sent until it is fixed or removed. The voter key file's locking and safe writing now live in one shared place; voting behaves as before.
