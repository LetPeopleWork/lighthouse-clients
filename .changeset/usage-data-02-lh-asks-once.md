`lh` now asks once, after a command's answer, whether to share anonymous usage data with your Lighthouse; nothing is sent until you say yes. Only `y` or `yes`, in any case, counts as a yes; Enter or anything else is a No that is kept for that Lighthouse and never sent. The question appears only when stdin, stdout and stderr are all a terminal (never in scripts, pipes or CI, and never under `DO_NOT_TRACK` set to anything but `0` or `false`), on stderr, so the command's output is unchanged. After a yes, `lh forecast manual` is counted as a manual forecast run from the command line. `lh` asks only after a command that reached Lighthouse and succeeded (never after help, `lh config`, `lh connection` or a refused command), only a Lighthouse that may be asked and labels the command line, and each Lighthouse on its own; a Lighthouse that does not answer about usage data costs at most a second, and the question waits for the next run. If your Lighthouse cannot record a yes, `lh` says so on stderr, keeps nothing and sends nothing, and the command's exit code is unchanged. Answers are kept in `usage-data.json` beside the command line's config, readable by you only and separate from the voter keys; when two terminals answer at once, the first answer given is kept, and an answers file `lh` cannot read is never written over: nothing is asked or sent until it is fixed or removed. A yes lasts across long gaps without a new question: `lh` checks it with your Lighthouse at most once a day, and renews a grant your Lighthouse let lapse on its own. A Lighthouse that no longer labels the command line gets nothing, and your yes is kept for when it does again. After a No, nothing is ever sent. Usage data never changes what `lh` prints or its exit code, in `--json`, `--toon` or `--pretty`, and a Lighthouse that never takes the events delays the run by at most a second. `lh config usage-data` shows what you answered for the Lighthouse you are connected to (`on`, `off` or `not asked yet (off)`) and whether that Lighthouse takes usage data from `lh`, its administrator has stopped it, it predates it, or it could not be asked within a second; under `DO_NOT_TRACK` it says so too. It asks nothing and sends nothing, and it is listed in `lh config`'s help. `lh config usage-data off` turns usage data off for that Lighthouse: it withdraws an earlier yes there, forgets the token and keeps a No, so nothing more is sent. When your Lighthouse cannot be told within a second, `off` holds all the same and says so; the yes it holds lapses by itself within 30 days. `off` is always allowed, also before anyone asked, under an administrator's stop and under `DO_NOT_TRACK`. Both commands need a connection, refuse an answers file they cannot read by naming it and leave it as it was, and anything else after `lh config usage-data` is refused with the group's help. The voter key file's locking and safe writing now live in one shared place; voting behaves as before.
